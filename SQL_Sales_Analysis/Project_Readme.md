# Contoso Sales Analysis — PostgreSQL SQL Portfolio

A hands-on SQL analytics project using the Contoso sales dataset. I used PostgreSQL to investigate five business questions across customer cohorts, customer value, customer retention, product category performance, and geographic markets. The goal was to practise translating business questions into readable queries, interpreting results, and communicating what the data does—and does not—show.

> **Project scope:** This is a learning portfolio based on the supplied Contoso dataset and query exports. Monetary figures are shown in the dataset's reporting units; the CSV exports do not specify a currency symbol, so none is assumed here.

## Questions explored

| # | Analysis | Main question | Techniques practised |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cohort analysis | How do customer counts and revenue recorded on the first-purchase date vary by acquisition cohort? | Aggregation, `GROUP BY`, filtering, calculated metrics |
| 2 | Customer segmentation | How is observed-period customer revenue distributed across low-, medium-, and high-value segments? | CTEs, percentile cutoffs, `CASE`, aggregation |
| 3 | Customer retention | How many eligible customers from each cohort are active or churned under a six-month inactivity rule? | CTEs, `ROW_NUMBER()`, `CASE`, window aggregates |
| 4 | Product category analysis | How do categories compare by units sold, revenue share, and average revenue per unit? | `JOIN`, aggregation, `RANK()`, `SUM() OVER()` |
| 5 | Geographic market performance | Which countries contribute the most revenue, and how has annual revenue changed? | Date extraction, CTEs, `RANK()`, `LAG()`, window aggregates, percentage calculations |

## Key findings

### 1. Cohort analysis


- The **2019 cohort** has the highest `total_revenue` in this export at approximately **22.25 million** across **7,755 customers**.
- The **2022 cohort** has the largest recorded customer count (**9,010**) and revenue of approximately **21.51 million**.
- Average revenue on the first-purchase date is highest for the **2016 cohort** at approximately **2,896.42 per customer**.

**Interpretation note:** Query 1 filters to `orderdate = first_purchase_date`. Therefore, `total_revenue` represents revenue recorded on customers' first-purchase dates, not all revenue generated by those cohorts over their subsequent lifetimes. The 2024 cohort may cover less than a full year, so validate date coverage before comparing it directly with earlier cohorts.


### 2. Customer segmentation


Customer-level revenue over the observed dataset period was segmented using the 25th and 75th percentiles.

- The **high-value segment** contains **12,372 customers** and contributes approximately **65.6%** of the segment revenue (about **135.43 million**).
- The **mid-value segment** contains **24,743 customers** and contributes approximately **32.3%**.
- The **low-value segment** contains **12,372 customers** and contributes approximately **2.1%**.

**Interpretation note:** The query calls summed customer revenue `total_ltv`, but it represents revenue observed in this dataset period. It is not a forecast of customers' future lifetime value.


### 3. Customer retention


The query identifies each customer's last purchase date and labels the customer as **churned** when their last purchase was more than six months before the latest sales date in the dataset; otherwise, they are **active**. It excludes customers whose first purchase occurred inside the most recent six-month window, so newer customers are not evaluated before they have had six months of opportunity to return.

- Across the eligible cohorts in the exported counts, active customers range from **8.4% to 10.4%** of each cohort's eligible customers.
- The **2022 cohort** has the highest active-customer count in this output (**937**).

**Percentage precision note:** The supplied query rounds the ratio before multiplying by 100, which makes its exported `status_percentage` values coarse approximations. The chart recalculates the shares from the exported active/churned counts so the proportions are more precise; the counts themselves are unchanged.


### 4. Product category analysis


- **Computers** ranks first in both sales volume and revenue, with **146,332 units**, approximately **90.62 million** in net revenue, and **43.90%** of category revenue.
- **Cell phones** ranks second in revenue and contributes **15.81%**.
- **Music, Movies and Audio Books** ranks **3rd** by units sold but **6th** by revenue, showing that volume rank and revenue rank can differ across categories.

**Interpretation note:** `avg_revenue_per_unit` is category net revenue divided by total units sold, so it is a blended average across that category's product mix—not the price of a typical individual item. Revenue is not profit because product costs are not included.


### 5. Geographic market performance


- The **United States (US)** contributes **51.86%** of total revenue in the export. **Canada (CA)** contributes **13.48%**, and **Germany (DE)** contributes **9.49%**.
- The top three markets together contribute approximately **74.83%** of total revenue; the top five contribute approximately **91.00%**.
- US revenue peaks in the exported annual series at approximately **23.74 million in 2022**.

**Year-coverage caution:** Revenue in 2024 is much lower than in 2023 across every listed country. This could reflect an incomplete 2024 period in the extract rather than a full-year decline. Check `MAX(orderdate)` and confirm the reporting period before interpreting the 2024 percentages as a business downturn. `LAG()` compares to the previous available year; the exported data has records for consecutive years for the listed countries.


## SQL concepts practised

- Aggregation with `SUM`, `COUNT`, and `GROUP BY`
- Joining sales records to customer and product information
- Creating and reusing a CTE/view for customer purchase history and cohort attributes
- Percentile-based customer segmentation with `CASE`
- `ROW_NUMBER()` to identify each customer's latest purchase
- Window aggregates such as `SUM(...) OVER ()`
- `RANK()` to compare country and product-category performance
- `LAG()` to compare a country's revenue with its previous available year
- Revenue contribution, average revenue per unit, and percentage-change calculations
- Exporting results to CSV and interpreting limitations in the output

## How to run the queries

1. Load the Contoso dataset into PostgreSQL. The queries expect `sales`, `customer`, and `product` tables in the `public` schema.
2. Run `00_create_cohort_analysis_view.sql` first. Queries 1–3 depend on `public.cohort_analysis`.
3. Run the five analysis queries in order, using DBeaver or another PostgreSQL SQL editor.
4. Export each result to CSV and compare it with the included output.

## Limitations and interpretation notes

- This is a learning project using the supplied dataset, not a comprehensive audit of Contoso's business.
- The cohort query measures revenue recorded on the first-purchase date, not full cohort lifetime revenue.
- Segment `total_ltv` reflects observed-period revenue and is not a forecast.
- Retention applies a six-month inactivity rule and excludes customers acquired inside the most recent six-month window; totals therefore represent eligible customers.
- Retention chart percentages are recalculated from the output counts because the original query rounds the ratio before multiplying by 100.
- The geographic output shows sharp drops in 2024 across markets. Confirm that the year is complete before interpreting those changes.
- These charts describe patterns in the provided exports; they do not establish causation.
